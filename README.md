# Moving to Ireland 🇮🇪

A free, community-edited checklist for people moving to Ireland, from your first week to settling in.

> **This guide is not legal or immigration advice.** Rules change. Always check the official source linked in each step. If something here is out of date, [please open a PR](CONTRIBUTING.md).

## 1. Before you arrive

- [ ] Check whether you need a visa or employment permit: [Irish Immigration Service](https://www.irishimmigration.ie/)
- [ ] EU/EEA/Swiss and UK citizens can live and work in Ireland without a permit.
- [ ] Bring originals of passport, birth/marriage certificates, degree certificates and driving licence.
- [ ] Ask your previous landlord or employer for a **reference letter**. Irish landlords often ask for one.
- [ ] Get a few months of **bank statements** from home; some banks and landlords want proof of funds.
- [ ] Book short-term accommodation for at least the first 2–4 weeks. Long-term rentals are hard to find from abroad.

## 2. First week

- [ ] Get an Irish SIM card (see [section 10](#10-phone-internet-and-utilities)). Many services need an Irish number.
- [ ] Get a **Leap Card** for public transport in Dublin, Cork, Galway, Limerick and Waterford: [leapcard.ie](https://www.leapcard.ie/)
- [ ] Get proof of address. A letter from your employer, a utility bill or a tenancy agreement usually works.
- [ ] Book your PPS Number appointment (next section). Slots can take weeks.

## 3. PPS Number

Your Personal Public Service Number is needed for work, tax, healthcare and social welfare.

- [ ] Book an appointment through [MyWelfare.ie](https://www.mywelfare.ie/). You'll need a basic MyGovID account.
- [ ] Bring photo ID, proof of address and a reason you need the number (e.g. a job offer).
- [ ] Your PPSN is posted to you, usually within a few days to a couple of weeks.
- [ ] Then upgrade to a **verified MyGovID**. It unlocks Revenue, HSE and other online services.

Official info: [Citizens Information – PPS Number](https://www.citizensinformation.ie/en/social-welfare/irish-social-welfare-system/personal-public-service-number/)

## 4. Bank account

- [ ] Main banks: AIB, Bank of Ireland, PTSB. Digital options like Revolut or N26 are widely used and quicker to open.
- [ ] Your employer needs an **IBAN** for salary. A Revolut or N26 IBAN may start with LT or DE, which some employers and landlords can't accept, so check first.
- [ ] Expect to show ID and proof of address.

## 5. Immigration registration (non-EEA)

- [ ] If you're staying more than 90 days, register your permission and get an **IRP (Irish Residence Permit)** card: [Irish Immigration Service – IRP](https://www.irishimmigration.ie/registering-your-immigration-permission/information-on-registering/irish-residence-permit/)
- [ ] Since January 2025, first-time registration for all counties is handled by Immigration Service Delivery. You book through their online portal, and the in-person appointment is in Dublin.
- [ ] Check the [required documents](https://www.irishimmigration.ie/registering-your-immigration-permission/how-to-register-your-immigration-permission-for-the-first-time/required-documents/). Private medical insurance is usually one of them.
- [ ] Keep your IRP card valid. You can renew online up to 12 weeks before it expires.

## 6. Renting a home

- [ ] Search: [daft.ie](https://www.daft.ie/), [myhome.ie](https://www.myhome.ie/), [rent.ie](https://www.rent.ie/)
- [ ] **Never pay a deposit before viewing** a property or confirming the landlord is real. Rental scams are common.
- [ ] Check the tenancy is registered with the [RTB (Residential Tenancies Board)](https://www.rtb.ie/). It also explains your rights.
- [ ] Deposits are usually one month's rent. In most cases landlords can't ask for more than one month's deposit and one month in advance.
- [ ] You may be able to claim **Rent Tax Credit** on your tax: [Revenue](https://www.revenue.ie/en/personal-tax-credits-reliefs-and-exemptions/housing/rent-tax-credit/index.aspx)

## 7. Working and tax

- [ ] Register your job on [Revenue myAccount](https://www.revenue.ie/en/online-services/services/manage-your-record/myaccount.aspx) using your PPSN. Without this you may be taxed at **emergency tax** rates.
- [ ] Check your tax credits are allocated to your main employment.
- [ ] Payslips show PAYE (income tax), USC and PRSI deductions.
- [ ] Learn your employment rights: [Workplace Relations Commission](https://www.workplacerelations.ie/)

## 8. Healthcare and GP

- [ ] Find a GP and register early. Many practices have waiting lists: [HSE service finder](https://www2.hse.ie/services/find-a-gp/)
- [ ] GP visits usually cost money unless you hold a **Medical Card** or **GP Visit Card**: [HSE eligibility](https://www2.hse.ie/services/schemes-allowances/)
- [ ] Children under a certain age get free GP care, and the age limits change, so check the HSE site.
- [ ] Consider private health insurance (VHI, Laya, Irish Life Health, Level).
- [ ] Join the **Drug Payment Scheme**, which caps monthly prescription costs per household.
- [ ] Emergency: dial **112** or **999**.

## 9. Driving

- [ ] Whether you can **exchange** your foreign licence depends on the issuing country: [NDLS – Exchange a licence](https://www.ndls.ie/)
- [ ] If you can't exchange it, you need a learner permit, lessons (EDT) and a driving test.
- [ ] Ireland drives on the **left**. Speed limits are in km/h.
- [ ] Car insurance can be expensive for newcomers without Irish history. A no-claims letter from your previous insurer can help.

## 10. Phone, internet and utilities

- [ ] Mobile: Vodafone, Three, Eir, plus lower-cost options like GoMo, 48 and Tesco Mobile.
- [ ] Broadband: check availability by Eircode on provider sites or [National Broadband Ireland](https://nbi.ie/).
- [ ] Electricity and gas: compare suppliers on [bonkers.ie](https://www.bonkers.ie/) or [switcher.ie](https://switcher.ie/).
- [ ] TV licence is required if you have a TV: [tvlicence.ie](https://www.tvlicence.ie/)

## 11. Children and school

- [ ] Primary schools: apply directly to the school; many have waiting lists.
- [ ] Free pre-school (**ECCE scheme**) for eligible children: [ECCE info](https://www.gov.ie/en/department-of-children-disability-and-equality/publications/early-childhood-care-and-education-programme-ecce/)
- [ ] Child Benefit may be available: [Citizens Information – Child Benefit](https://www.citizensinformation.ie/en/social-welfare/families-and-children/child-benefit/)
- [ ] Irish language may be required in school. Exemptions exist for children who arrive after a certain age.
